COVID-19 in Bangladesh vs. the World — an April 2020 Snapshot

A geospatial comparison of the global spread of COVID-19 with the situation in Bangladesh and Dhaka during the earliest phase of the pandemic.

Background

In April 2020 I worked with GroupMappers and the MORU Tropical Health Network to produce fast, clear geospatial communication. The map had one job: help decision-makers see how Bangladesh's case numbers compared globally, how cases were distributed nationally, and how heavily they were concentrated in Dhaka.

Design decisions

  • Proportional symbols in a graduated yellow-to-red scheme
  • Inset maps for Europe, Bangladesh and Dhaka City
  • Colour-blind-friendly classes with a clear size hierarchy
  • Comparison boxes giving global context at a glance
  • A direct public message: "Stay Home! Stay Safe!"

Tools

ArcGIS Desktop (ArcMap) · Adobe Illustrator · Excel for data cleaning

The Spark of Demographics: Mapping Population in Bangladesh

A population density map built on 2022 BBS census data, showing just how sharply Bangladesh's population concentrates around Dhaka.

Overview

The map shows population distribution at union/upazila level from BBS 2022 data — Dhaka's overwhelming dominance, dense settlement along major river corridors, and the contrast between the crowded east and centre and the sparser north.

Cartographic choices

  • Proportional symbols — glowing red circles sized by population
  • A dark base with luminous points for a "spark" effect
  • Deliberate visual hierarchy so Dhaka reads first
  • Rivers in light blue to show how physical geography shapes settlement

Tools

ArcGIS Pro / ArcMap · Adobe Illustrator · Excel / Google Sheets

Insight: the dark-base, glowing-point style has worked especially well for social media and public presentations.

"In a country of 170 million people living in just 147,570 km², population mapping is not just cartography — it is storytelling."

Featured Visualisation

Spatiotemporal Land Use Dynamics

Thirty years of landscape transformation as an interactive flow — how land cover moved between classes from 1995 to 2025.

What is a Sankey diagram?

A flow chart where ribbon width is proportional to quantity. Each ribbon shows how much of one land use class — say, agricultural land — became another, such as aquaculture or urban settlement, over a decade. Thicker ribbons mean larger conversions.

How to read it

Four time columns — 1995 → 2005 → 2015 → 2025. Each coloured block is a class in that year; ribbons between columns are transitions. Hover over any block or ribbon for its label, share or exact value. Colours stay consistent across years.

What it reveals

Agricultural land fell from 19.4% to 11.0% while aquaculture rose from 15.8% to 21.0%. Urban settlement more than doubled (4.2% → 10.5%) and rural homestead grew from 8.2% to 12.0% — signs of rising population pressure and a changing coastal economy.
